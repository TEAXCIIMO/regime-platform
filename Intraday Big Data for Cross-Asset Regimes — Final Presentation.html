Motivation
Three research questions
RQ1
Do intraday features detect regimes better than daily features alone?
Test: how well the regime score separates SPY volatility over the next 5 days.
RQ2
Do regime signals add value over 60/40 and placebo signals, after costs?
Test: net Sharpe on a sealed 2024–2026 holdout, with bootstrap intervals.
RQ3
What matters most: asset breadth, data frequency or model complexity?
Test: spread of Sharpe across the levels of each design factor.

Verdict

First signal at noon

Limitations
What limits these conclusions
Statistical power
710 holdout days cannot separate a 0.2 Sharpe difference from zero.
Compute quota
16 vCPU limit: scaling measured on 2, 4 and 7 two-core workers, not 1, 4 and 16 four-core.
Illiquid currency ETFs
Five FX ETFs rarely trade each minute, so the FX volatility feature was dropped.
Regime-quality metric
Log-likelihoods over different feature spaces are not comparable, so RQ1 rests on volatility separation and Brier score.
Conclusions
Better regimes, no proven portfolio gain
RQ1 · partly
Intraday features nearly double volatility separation (−0.43 vs −0.24).
RQ2 · not supported
Holdout Sharpe 0.88 vs 0.72, but every 95% interval includes zero.
RQ3 · complexity
States and refit frequency matter more than data frequency or breadth.
A reproducible platform: Spark batch, BigQuery, Pub/Sub streaming and a Streamlit dashboard, for about $3 of cloud time.
